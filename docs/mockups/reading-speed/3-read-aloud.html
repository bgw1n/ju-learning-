The little island was quiet until the wolves arrived. At first they walked the rocky shore and sniffed the wind. Then they made a den under a fallen log. Soon the pack was hunting fish, crabs, and even the birds that nested in the tall grass.

Every animal on the island felt the change. The deer hid in the trees. The rabbits dug deeper burrows. Even the foxes watched from a distance. But the wolves did more than hunt. Their leftover meals fed beetles and flies. Those bugs fed the birds. The birds dropped seeds, and new plants began to grow along the path. All this helped.

In time, the island was busier than ever. Plants covered the bare spots. Fish returned to the clear coves. Birds sang from the new bushes, and crabs scurried back under the wet rocks. The wolves had found a habitat, and the habitat had found a way to share it. One spring morning, two wolf pups tumbled out of the den and blinked at the sun. The island, once empty, was a home again.
